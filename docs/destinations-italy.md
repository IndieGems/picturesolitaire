# Italy: candidate destinations

Trip 3, boards 31–45 (creator, 2026-10-26). Each board has one real place, shown as a
postcard the size of the whole board. A 2×2 part of it, the **crop**, plays on the board.
The suggested 15 at the end are made; the creator swaps any after seeing them. How
postcards are made is in `docs/picture-direction.md` ("Destinations").

**Shapes.**
- Boards 31–35 and 37–45 are 4×5, so their postcards are a tall 3:5.
- Board 36 is the trip's all-long board: a 4×4 postcard that plays as a two-card strip.

**Tags.** *famous* is a place everyone knows. *hidden gem* is a place few players will
know but they'll want to visit. *silly* is a delightful oddity. The mix is 8 famous, 4
gems and 3 silly.

**Italy's rights rule, first.** Italy's Cultural Heritage Code needs permission, and a fee,
to reproduce commercially a cultural property held by the state or another public body:
monuments, archaeological sites, museum works. It is enforced even against a generated
picture. A Venice court ruled against the puzzle maker Ravensburger over its *Vitruvian
Man* puzzle.
- **So the trip leans to landscapes, coasts, ordinary towns and private places.** It leaves
  out any picture built around a state or public monument.
- **Churches belong to their dioceses,** which control their own images. A church small in
  a landscape is low risk; a church as the subject is left out.
- **No writing:** shop signs, boat names and plaques are kept out.

**Library look-alikes.** These postcards take the family of an everyday picture like them,
so the two never share a board:
- Venice and Lake Como take *Canal Lights* / *Lakeside Evening*.
- Positano takes *Lemon Coast*.
- Cinque Terre takes *Harbour at Dusk*.
- Val d'Orcia takes *Wheat and Cypress*.
- Burano takes *Pastel Row*.
- Capri takes *Sea Stack*.
- Isola Bella takes *Peacock*.

*Venice Canal* and *Lemon Coast* are also marked as landmarks, so they never play on a
campaign board.

**Tested.** The 15 suggested places are made and were each checked against Wikipedia or
Commons photos. The other 10 are judged by how well known and how often photographed they
are.

## Left out, and why

- **The Colosseum, Pompeii, the Roman Forum and Sicily's Valley of the Temples.**
  State-owned archaeological sites: the Heritage Code's permission is needed.
- **The Leaning Tower and the Field of Miracles in Pisa.** A monument complex under
  heritage protection, and *Leaning Tower* is a library landmark already.
- **Florence's Duomo, St Mark's, St Peter's and the Sistine Chapel.** Church and state
  monuments.
- **The Trevi Fountain, the Spanish Steps and the Rialto Bridge.** Public monuments of
  their cities.
- **Michelangelo's David and the Vitruvian Man.** State-held works: the case that started
  the caution.
- **Sirmione's castle and Castel del Monte.** State-owned castles.
- **The Scala dei Turchi.** The white cliff is now public property and closed to visitors
  after damage.
- **Pinocchio Park and La Scarzuola.** Modern sculpture and architecture by named artists,
  and Pinocchio's best-known look is Disney's.
- **The Big Benches of the Langhe.** A designer's trademarked project.
