# New Zealand: candidate destinations

Trip 11, boards 151–165 (creator, 2026-11-07). The trip adds no new art except its postcards
and its stamp: every everyday picture comes from the library. Each board has one real place,
shown as a postcard the size of the whole board. A 2×2 part of it, the **crop**, plays on the
board. How postcards are made is in `docs/picture-direction.md` ("Destinations").

**Shapes.** Levels is setting the slots (`docs/difficulty-curve.md`, "Trips 11-15 (plan)").
Until then they are assumed to be trip 9's:
- Most boards are 4×5, with a tall 3:5 postcard.
- The x10 board (160) and the finale (165) are 5×5, with a 3:4 postcard. Only every other
  finale is 6×6 (150, 180).
- 156 is the all-long board: a 4×4 postcard that plays as a two-card strip.

**Tags.** *famous* is a place everyone knows. *hidden gem* is a place few players will know
but they'll want to visit. *silly* is something funny or odd. The mix is 8 famous, 4 gems and
3 silly, from both islands: North 4, South 11.

**The rights rules, first** (checked 2026-11-07).
- **Freedom of panorama covers buildings, and sculptures and works of artistic craftsmanship
  permanently in a public place** (Copyright Act 1994, s 73). It does not cover murals or other
  flat art. That covers Napier's 1930s buildings, Baldwin Street's houses and Ohakune's carrot.
- **No mountain or water that is an ancestor or tapu.** That rules out:
  - Aoraki/Mount Cook, Ngāi Tahu's ancestor, and the views built round it (Lake Matheson's
    mirror, Lake Pukaki, the Hooker Valley);
  - Taranaki, a legal person since 2025 (Te Kāhui Tupua);
  - Tongariro, Ngauruhoe and Ruapehu, and the Emerald Lakes, whose waters local iwi ask
    visitors not to touch;
  - the Whanganui River, a legal person since 2017;
  - Te Rerenga Wairua (Cape Reinga), where spirits depart;
  - Tāne Mahuta, the great kauri that Te Roroa treat as a living ancestor.
- **A legend told openly is not a sacred site.** The Moeraki Boulders' story, food baskets
  washed from the Āraiteuru canoe, is told to every visitor, like Finn McCool at the Giant's
  Causeway. Franz Josef's story of Hine Hukatere's tears is the same.
- **No Māori carving, marae, waka, tā moko or pounamu designs** on any postcard. They are
  taonga, and none is drawn as decoration.
- **Rotorua's Pōhutu is free to picture as a generated view.** It stands in Te Puia's grounds,
  beside the living village of Whakarewarewa. Te Puia's permissions are for filming and
  photography on site, like the National Trust's. So it is shown across the silica terraces,
  with no carvings, gateway, village, graves, boardwalk crowds or people.
- **Waitomo's glowworm grotto is out.** Photography is forbidden there, and it belongs to the
  Ruapuha Uekaha hapū.
- **No film tie-ins.** Hobbiton is out, and so are Mount Sunday and every other place sold as
  a film location.
- **Trade marks and brands are kept out:** the Sky Tower, SkyCity's; Paeroa's L&P bottle;
  every sign, logo, tour boat livery and car badge. Ohakune's carrot was a 1984 prop for an ANZ
  Bank advert, and no ANZ mark appears with it.
- **No modern art:** Kawakawa's Hundertwasser toilets are out.
- **No tragedy:** Whakaari/White Island, after the 2019 eruption.
- **No people:** every postcard shows a place, an animal or both. That leaves out Hot Water
  Beach, whose joke is the people digging pools.

**Library look-alikes.** These postcards take the family of an everyday picture like them,
so the two never share a board:
- Milford Sound takes *Fjord Evening*'s family (blue-hour), as Lake Como and Venice did.
- Lake Tekapo takes *Lupins*.
- Pōhutu takes *Geyser*.
- Napier takes *Art Deco*.
- The Wānaka tree takes *Lone Tree*.
- The kiwi takes *Kiwi* (the bird).
- Kaikōura's seals take *Seal Pup*.
- Arrowtown takes *Maple Path*'s family (autumn-rust), as the Cabot Trail did.
- The Pancake Rocks take *Sea Stack*.
- Curio Bay takes *Petrified Wood*.
- Ohakune's carrot takes *Pulling Carrots*.

**Earlier postcards it avoids repeating.**
- Lighthouses (four already), so Nugget Point is a reserve.
- Whales (Argentina), so Kaikōura shows its seals.
- Gannets (Canada), so Cape Kidnappers is left out.
- A coloured hot spring (Grand Prismatic), so Wai-O-Tapu's Champagne Pool is left out.
- Sea arches (three already), so Cathedral Cove is a reserve.
- Ohakune's carrot is the Big Banana's cousin, but the creator asked for it, and the giant
  roadside thing is a running joke of the trips.

## Left out, and why

- **Aoraki/Mount Cook, Taranaki, Tongariro and the Emerald Lakes, the Whanganui River, Te
  Rerenga Wairua and Tāne Mahuta:** ancestors, legal persons or tapu.
- **Waitomo's glowworm grotto:** no photography, and the hapū's own.
- **Hobbiton and Mount Sunday:** film tie-ins.
- **The Sky Tower and Paeroa's L&P bottle:** trade marks.
- **Kawakawa's Hundertwasser toilets:** modern art.
- **Whakaari/White Island:** the 2019 eruption.
- **Wai-O-Tapu's Champagne Pool and Cape Kidnappers' gannets:** too like Grand Prismatic and
  Bonaventure's gannets.
- **Hot Water Beach:** its joke needs people.

That is 4 oil paintings, 3 travel posters, 3 watercolours, 3 gouaches and 2 pastels. The
styles share the place's culture and period:
- The travel posters follow the 1930s Government Tourist Bureau posters, which made Rotorua,
  Napier and the West Coast famous.
- The oils follow the colonial landscape painters of the lakes and the Sounds.
- The watercolours follow the 19th-century survey watercolours.

The board shapes follow Levels' slots.
