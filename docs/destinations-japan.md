# Japan: candidate destinations

Trip 2, boards 16–30 (creator, 2026-10-25). Each board has one real place, shown as a
postcard the size of the whole board. A 2×2 part of it, the **crop**, plays on the board.
The suggested 15 at the end are already made, as the lead asked: the creator can swap any
of them after seeing them. How postcards are made, and the rights rules, are in
`docs/destinations-america.md` and `docs/picture-direction.md` ("Destinations").

**Shapes.**
- Boards 16–21 are 4×4, so their postcards are 3:4.
- Board 21 is the trip's all-long board, so its postcard plays as a wide 2-card strip.
- Boards 22–30 are 4×5, so their postcards are a taller 3:5 and suit tall subjects:
  waterfalls, pagodas, torii and castles.

**Tags.** *famous* is a place everyone knows. *hidden gem* is a place few players will
know but they'll want to visit. *silly* is a delightful oddity. The creator's mix is 8
famous, 4 gems and 3 silly.

**Tested.**
- **The 15 suggested places are made.** Each was checked against Wikipedia or Commons
  photos of the place.
- **One was corrected.** At Chureito, Fuji stands to the left of the pagoda, and the
  picture came out mirrored. It is flipped now.
- **Others are judged.** The 10 places not made are judged by how well known and how
  often photographed they are.

**Rights to watch in Japan.**
- **Licensed images.** Tokyo Tower's and Tokyo Skytree's images are licensed by their
  owners.
- **No characters or mascots.** No Hello Kitty, Ghibli, Pokémon, Gundam or Kumamon.
- **Writing.** Shrines and streets carry a lot of writing: donor names on torii, plaques,
  signs and shop curtains. Every postcard keeps them blank.
- **Shrines and temples.** Many ask commercial photographers for permission. A generated
  picture is not their photo, but keep it respectful: no jokes, no characters, no
  writing.
- **Sacred places with rules of their own** are left out (see below).

**Library look-alikes.**
- *Torii* (marked a landmark, so it's off campaign boards anyway) is close to Itsukushima.
- *Pagoda* is close to the Chureito pagoda.
- *Ribbon Falls* is close to Nachi Falls and Takachiho.
- *Rabbit* is close to Rabbit Island.
- *Rope Bridge* is close to the Iya vine bridge.

Each of those postcards takes that picture's family, so they never share a board.
*Mount Fuji* is marked a landmark and never plays as an everyday picture.

## Left out, and why

- **Tokyo Tower and Tokyo Skytree.** Their images are licensed by their owners.
- **Shibuya Crossing and Dōtonbori.** Signs, billboards and the Glico running man (a
  trademark).
- **Characters and mascots.** Hello Kitty, Ghibli, Pokémon, Gundam, Kumamon.
- **Hiroshima's Atomic Bomb Dome and Gunkanjima.** A memorial, and an island with a
  forced-labour history: not a game's backdrop.
- **Ise Grand Shrine and Okinoshima.** Ise's inner shrine may not be photographed, and
  Okinoshima is closed to visitors.
- **Naoshima's yellow pumpkin and teamLab.** Modern art by living artists.
- **Ushiku Daibutsu.** A modern religious statue (1993).
- **Shigaraki tanuki.** The traditional figures have exaggerated anatomy: not for all
  ages.
- **Hitachi Seaside Park, Tottori Sand Dunes and Ginzan Onsen.** For the first two,
  every card would be blue flowers or sand. Ginzan Onsen's inn fronts are covered in
  writing.
