# South Korea: candidate destinations

Trip 19, boards 271–285 (creator, 2026-11-08). Like the other trips from 17 to 22, it adds no
new art except its postcards and its stamp. Each board has one real place, shown as a postcard
the size of the whole board. A 2×2 part of it, the **crop**, plays on the board. How postcards
are made is in `docs/picture-direction.md` ("Destinations").

**Shapes** (Levels, 2026-11-08):
- Most boards are 4×5, with a tall 3:5 postcard.
- 275, the hard board, is 5×5, with a 3:4 postcard.
- The finale, 285, is 6×6, with a 3:4 postcard.
- 276 is the all-long board: a 4×4 postcard whose window is a wide two-card strip.

**Tags.** *famous* is a place everyone knows. *hidden gem* is a place few players will know
but they'll want to visit. *silly* is something funny or odd. The mix is 8 famous, 4 gems and
3 silly. The trip runs from Seoul down the west coast to the south, along to Busan, up through
the old kingdom of Silla to the mountains of Gangwon, and ends on Jeju. That is 10 of the
country's provinces and cities, with no more than three boards in any one.

**The rights rules, first** (checked 2026-11-08).
- **Korea's freedom of panorama is narrow.** The Copyright Act (Art. 35(2)) lets anyone
  reproduce works on permanent public display, but not "for the purpose of selling copies". So
  no modern building or artwork appears:
  - Lotte World Tower, the Dongdaemun Design Plaza and the Gwangan Bridge;
  - Gwanghwamun Plaza's statues of King Sejong (2009) and Admiral Yi (1968);
  - Gamcheon's murals and installations, including its Little Prince statue, licensed from the
    Saint-Exupéry estate.

  Only old buildings, ordinary houses and nature appear.
- **Heritage sites** (the Korea Heritage Service) need a permit to film or photograph for
  commercial use on site. We do neither: every postcard is painted from words. The palace is seen
  from the public plaza, as the gate faces the street.
- **N Seoul Tower is out:** a company's (CJ's) landmark and brand. So are Lotte World, Everland,
  O'sulloc, Seoul's Haechi mascot and Pengsoo.
- **Drama and K-pop tie-ins are out:**
  - Nami Island (*Winter Sonata*, and a company's island);
  - the BTS bus stop at Jumunjin, and *Goblin*'s breakwater;
  - Cheongsando (*Seopyeonje*);
  - the floating hermitage at Jusanji, a film set.
- **No temples, shrines or tombs:**
  - Bulguksa, Seokguram, Haedong Yonggungsa, Jogyesa, Maisan's stone towers and Seoraksan's
    Sinheungsa with its giant Buddha are out;
  - so are Jongmyo, the royal ancestral shrine, and the royal tombs, including Gyeongju's
    burial mounds next to Cheomseongdae, which stay out of frame;
  - Gochang's dolmens are graves.
  - **Jeju's dol hareubang are out.** These stone "grandfathers" are guardian deities of
    protection and fertility, so they count as sacred. Village guardian poles (*jangseung*) and
    shrine trees are out for the same reason, Hahoe's sacred tree among them.
- **No borders or maps:**
  - the DMZ, Panmunjom, Imjingak, Cheorwon's cranes and the islands on the Northern Limit Line;
  - Dokdo;
  - Yeongwol's river bend shaped like the whole peninsula.
- **No painful history:**
  - Jeju 4.3 sites, Gwangju's May 18 cemetery, Paengmok harbour (Sewol), Itaewon and Seodaemun
    Prison;
  - streets built under Japanese rule (Gunsan, Mokpo, Guryongpo).
- **No flags, scripts or seals:** no Taegukgi, and no Hangul or hanja anywhere. The palace gate's
  name board is blank, and the paintings carry no seals or inscriptions.
- **No people:** the haenyeo divers, mask dances, markets, the palace guard ceremony and
  Bukchon's residents. Bukchon now limits visitors' hours, so its lanes are painted empty.

**Library look-alikes.** These postcards take the family of an everyday picture like them,
so the two never share a board:
- Bukchon takes *Hanok Roof* (hanok-eaves).
- Gamcheon takes *Hillside Houses*.
- The reeds take *Reed Bed* (tall-grass).
- The onggi jars take *Kimchi Making*.
- Sancheong's persimmons take *Persimmons*.
- Jeju's tangerines take *Orange Grove*.
- Hahoe takes *Thatched Cottage*.

The other eight get families of their own. The tangerine card doesn't use the name
*hallabong*: whether it is a trade mark is unclear.

**Shipped postcards, not repeated.**
- **Hahoe is not Shirakawa-go.** Its thatch is low, rounded and golden, on single-storey houses
  in a river bend. Shirakawa-go has tall, steep A-frames.
- **Gamcheon is not Cinque Terre or Chefchaouen.** It is flat-roofed pastel boxes stacked up a
  hillside above Busan's harbour, not cliffside towers or blue lanes.
- **The persimmon curtains are not Hoi An's lanterns:** strings of fruit under drying-shed roofs,
  in the hills, not lanterns over a street.
- **Seongsan Ilchulbong is a green crater crown at the sea's edge**, not Payunia's desert cones.
- **Ulsanbawi is a long granite wall of rounded knobs above pine forest**, not Fitz Roy's spires
  or the Tre Cime. It is painted in summer, not autumn.
- **Hwahongmun is a fortress water gate:** seven low arches under a pavilion, with water pouring
  through. It isn't a viaduct, and Hwaseong's walls are left out so they don't foreshadow the
  Great Wall (trip 22).
- **Left out as look-alikes:**
  - Jinhae's cherry blossoms (the Tidal Basin), Boseong's tea fields (the library's *Tea
    Terraces*), the Inje birch forest (*Birches*) and Damyang's bamboo (*Bamboo*);
  - Gyeongbokgung's Gyeonghoeru and Changdeokgung's Buyongji, pavilions over pools like the
    Menara Gardens;
  - Jusangjeolli, basalt columns like the Giant's Causeway;
  - Udo's white sand and turquoise water, like Whitehaven;
  - Sinan's purple islands, a village painted one colour like Chefchaouen;
  - Daraengi's terraced paddies, like Sapa.

## Left out, and why

- **Modern buildings and art:** Lotte World Tower, the DDP, the Gwangan Bridge, the plaza
  statues, Gamcheon's murals and the Little Prince.
- **N Seoul Tower, Lotte World, Everland, O'sulloc, Haechi and Pengsoo:** brands.
- **Nami Island, Jumunjin, Cheongsando and the Jusanji hermitage:** drama, K-pop and film
  tie-ins.
- **Temples, shrines, royal tombs and dolmens, the dol hareubang, guardian poles and shrine
  trees:** sacred.
- **The DMZ, Panmunjom, Cheorwon, the NLL islands, Dokdo and the peninsula-shaped river:**
  borders and maps.
- **Jeju 4.3, Gwangju, Sewol, Itaewon, Seodaemun and the colonial streets:** painful history.
- **The haenyeo, mask dances, markets and the guard ceremony:** people.
- **Look-alikes:** Jinhae, Boseong, Inje, Damyang, Gyeonghoeru, Buyongji, Jusangjeolli, Udo,
  Sinan and Daraengi.

That is 4 ink-and-colour paintings, 3 folk paintings, 3 watercolours, 2 oil paintings, a gouache,
a pastel and a travel poster. Each style shares the place's culture and period, and none copies a
real picture:
- **The ink-and-colour paintings** follow Joseon painting, cleaned up: clean brush outlines
  and flat washes of soft mineral colour, without the dotting and texture strokes, seals or
  inscriptions.
  - Gwanghwamun follows the court's palace panoramas of the 1820s.
  - Hwahongmun follows the illustrated record of the fortress's building (1801).
  - Hahoe and Ulsanbawi follow the 18th-century "true-view" landscapes of Korea's own mountains
    and villages.
- **The folk paintings** follow Joseon *minhwa*: bold flat colour, dark outlines and a sense
  of fun. Fish, fruit and jars are among its everyday subjects.
- The watercolours, the gouache, the pastel and the oils follow Korean painting of the 20th
  century.
- The poster follows the mid-century decades in which Gamcheon was built.

These two Korean styles need two new style lines, *postcard-ink-colour* and *postcard-minhwa*.
China's postcards (trip 22) will use the first too.

**Stamp t19:** a magpie on a persimmon branch, the Korean good-news bird, in a ring of small
plum blossoms, in deep pine-green ink. That hue (about 130) is free between East Coast America's
leaf green (111) and New Zealand's emerald (148), and it will be darker than both.

**Cost:** 15 postcards at medium quality are about $0.30, and the stamp about $0.05. With a few
redraws the trip comes to about $0.45.
