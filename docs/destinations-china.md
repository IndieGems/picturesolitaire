# China: candidate destinations

Trip 22, boards 316–330 (creator, 2026-11-08), the last of the six trips that ship together.
Like the others, it adds no new art except its postcards and its stamp. Each board has one real
place, shown as a postcard the size of the whole board. A 2×2 part of it, the **crop**, plays
on the board. How postcards are made is in `docs/picture-direction.md` ("Destinations").

**Shapes** (Levels, 2026-11-08):
- Most boards are 4×5, with a tall 3:5 postcard. The finale, 330, is 4×5 too.
- 320 and 325, the hard boards, are 5×5, with a 3:4 postcard.
- 321 is the all-long board: a 4×4 postcard whose window is a tall two-card strip.

**Tags.** *famous* is a place everyone knows. *hidden gem* is a place few players will know
but they'll want to visit. *silly* is something funny or odd. The mix is 8 famous, 4 gems and
3 silly. The trip runs from the south coast north through the Jiangnan villages, Hunan's
mountains and Yunnan, then up to Xi'an and Shanxi, out to the grasslands and the north-east,
and ends on the Great Wall. That is 13 provinces, with no more than two boards in any one.

**The rights rules, first** (checked 2026-11-08).
- **China's freedom of panorama is wide.** The Copyright Law (2020, Art. 24(1)(10)) lets anyone
  copy, draw or photograph artistic works placed in outdoor public places, and courts have
  allowed commercial use. All the same, every building here is a Ming or Qing building, a
  village house or a tower of the early 1900s, and modern buildings stay out: Shanghai's
  skyline, the Bird's Nest, Chongqing's monorail building and Zhangjiajie's glass bridge.
- **Licensed images and trade marks.** The Forbidden City is out: the Palace Museum licenses
  its image and has registered its name. The Summer Palace is out for the same reason. Several
  scenic areas are run by tourism companies (Mutianyu, Hongcun, Huangling, Fenghuang and
  Zhangjiajie) and ask for permits to film or photograph commercially on site. Those are rules
  for cameras on the grounds, so these places follow the ruling on Poland's museums.
- **Film tie-ins:**
  - Zhangjiajie is named as itself, never as *Avatar*'s mountains. The park renamed a pillar
    for the film, and the card doesn't use that name.
  - Hongcun appears in *Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon*, and the tulou in Disney's *Mulan*. The
    cards name neither film.
  - Wulong's karst bridges (*Transformers*) are left out.
- **Banknote views are not copied:**
  - the Li River at Xingping (20 yuan);
  - West Lake's Three Pools Mirroring the Moon (1 yuan), so West Lake is painted from the
    Broken Bridge instead;
  - the Qutang Gorge (10 yuan) and the Potala (50 yuan).
- **No Chinese characters anywhere:** no signboards, couplets, banners, lanterns with
  writing, seals or inscriptions. The Bell Tower's name board and Pingyao's shop signs are
  painted blank or left out.
- **No sacred places or tombs:**
  - temples and pagodas, including Leifeng Pagoda at West Lake (out of frame), Shaolin, the
    Temple of Heaven, the Lama Temple, the Hanging Temple, the Mogao and Longmen caves, Leshan's
    Buddha and Shaxi's Xingjiao Temple, which faces its square and stays out;
  - ancestral halls (the central halls of the tulou are not shown), and the five sacred
    mountains with Wudang;
  - Jade Dragon Snow Mountain, sacred to the Naxi;
  - the Terracotta Army, the Ming Tombs and the hanging coffins.
- **No politics, borders or maps:** Tiananmen and Mao's image; Tibet, the Potala and the Tibetan
  areas of Sichuan (Jiuzhaigou); Xinjiang; Hong Kong, Macau and Taiwan; maps and frontiers. The
  Three Gorges Dam is out for its displaced towns.
- **Brands:** Tsingtao, Moutai and the WWF's panda logo.
- **No people:** cormorant fishermen, tea pickers, minority villagers in costume, crowds, and
  Harbin's ice festival.

**Library look-alikes.** These postcards take the family of an everyday picture like them,
so the two never share a board:
- Hongcun takes *River Village* (river-village-willows): white houses on water.
- West Lake takes *Stone Bridge* (arch-bridge).
- The golden monkeys take snow-monkeys, the family of Japan's postcard, so the two never share a
  board.
- The giant salamander takes *Fire Salamander*.
- The Xi'an Bell Tower takes *Pagoda*.
- Fenghuang takes *Stilt House*, Koh Panyee's family.
- Jilin's rime takes *Snowy Pine*.

The other eight get families of their own.

**Shipped postcards and library pictures, not repeated.**
- **The lead's ideas, vetted:**
  - The Great Wall is in, at Mutianyu.
  - **Guilin's karst on the Li River is out.** The library's *Karst Peaks* is that very picture,
    and Ha Long Bay (trip 15) is its sea twin.
  - **Pandas are out as a postcard:** the library's *Panda* is the same picture. The panda
    becomes the stamp instead.
- **Suzhou's and Wuzhen's canals are out:** the library's *River Village* is a Chinese water
  town.
- **Huangshan is out:** granite peaks and pines in a sea of clouds, like Ulsanbawi (trip 19).
- **Golden monkeys, not snow monkeys:** painted in green summer forest, golden-furred and
  blue-faced, never in snow or a hot spring.
- **Fenghuang is not Koh Panyee.** Its dark wooden stilt houses line a green river with an
  arched covered bridge, not a village over the sea under a karst.
- **The Bell Tower is a city tower on a brick base,** not Fuji's red pagoda among blossom.
- **Huangling's drying trays are round, flat and laid out on racks**, not hung in strings like
  Sancheong's persimmons.
- **Left out as look-alikes:**
  - Zhangye's rainbow hills, like the Painted Hills and Purmamarca;
  - the Longji and Yuanyang terraces, like Sapa;
  - Huanglong's pools, like Pamukkale;
  - the Stone Forest, like the Pinnacles;
  - Hukou and Huangguoshu, waterfalls;
  - Dunhuang's Crescent Lake, like Erg Chebbi;
  - Pingtan's glowing sea, like the library's *Glowing Bay*;
  - the Yu Garden's zigzag bridge and teahouse, and Suzhou's moon gates, which the library has.

## Left out, and why

- **The Forbidden City and the Summer Palace:** licensed images and trade marks.
- **Modern buildings:** Shanghai's skyline, the Bird's Nest, the monorail building and the glass
  bridge.
- ***Avatar*, *Mulan*, *Crouching Tiger* and *Transformers*:** not named.
- **Banknote views:** the Li River, the Three Pools, the Qutang Gorge and the Potala.
- **Temples, pagodas, caves, ancestral halls, sacred mountains and tombs:** sacred.
- **Tiananmen, Mao, Tibet, Xinjiang, Hong Kong, Macau, Taiwan, maps and the Three Gorges Dam:**
  politics and borders.
- **Tsingtao, Moutai and the WWF panda:** brands.
- **Fishermen, pickers, costumes, crowds and Harbin's festival:** people.
- **Look-alikes:** Guilin, pandas, Suzhou, Huangshan, Zhangye, the terraces, Huanglong, the
  Stone Forest, the waterfalls, Dunhuang, Pingtan and the Yu Garden.

That is 4 ink-and-colour paintings, 3 oil paintings, 2 gouaches, 2 travel posters, 2 watercolours
and 2 pastels. Each style shares the place's culture and period, and none copies a real picture:
- **The ink-and-colour paintings** reuse Korea's style line (*postcard-ink-colour*): clean
  outlines and flat mineral washes, with no texture strokes, seals or inscriptions.
  - West Lake and Zhangjiajie follow the Song dynasty's blue-and-green landscapes.
  - The monkeys follow the fine-line *gongbi* animal painting of the court.
  - Hongcun follows the Huizhou painters of its own region.
- The oils, watercolours, pastels and gouaches follow 20th-century Chinese painting of its own
  villages and countryside, after no named artist.
- The posters are mid-century travel illustration, with no slogans.

**Stamp t22:** a giant panda chewing bamboo, in a ring of small plum blossoms, in cinnabar ink:
the orange-red of Chinese seal paste. That hue (about 25) is free between the American West's
rust (14) and Australia's ochre (38).

**Cost:** 15 postcards at medium quality are about $0.30, and the stamp about $0.05. With a few
redraws the trip comes to about $0.45.
