# Türkiye: candidate destinations

Trip 21, boards 301–315 (creator, 2026-11-08; the trip is "Türkiye", with ü, in every language,
and the adjective stays "Turkish"). Like the other trips from 17 to 22, it adds no new art except
its postcards and its stamp. Each board has one real place, shown as a postcard the size of the
whole board. A 2×2 part of it, the **crop**, plays on the board. How postcards are made is in
`docs/picture-direction.md` ("Destinations").

**Shapes** (Levels, 2026-11-08):
- Most boards are 4×5, with a tall 3:5 postcard.
- 305, the hard board, is 5×5, with a 3:4 postcard.
- The finale, 315, is 6×6, with a 3:4 postcard.
- 306 is the all-long board: a 4×4 postcard whose window is a tall two-card strip.

**Tags.** *famous* is a place everyone knows. *hidden gem* is a place few players will know
but they'll want to visit. *silly* is something funny or odd. The mix is 8 famous, 4 gems and
3 silly. The trip starts in Istanbul. It goes east along the Black Sea to Van, back through the
Hittite heartland to the Aegean, along the Turquoise Coast, and ends among Cappadocia's balloons.
That is 11 provinces, with no more than three boards (Istanbul) in any one.

**The rights rules, first** (checked 2026-11-08).
- **Freedom of panorama.** Türkiye's copyright law (Law 5846, Art. 40) lets anyone reproduce
  works permanently on public streets and squares by drawing, painting or photography. It covers
  buildings' outsides only, and commentators disagree whether it covers commercial use. So every
  building here is Ottoman or older and long out of copyright, and the Basilica Cistern, the one
  interior, dates from the 6th century.
- **Museums and excavations.** The Ministry of Culture and Tourism requires a permit to film or
  photograph commercially at museums and archaeological sites (Ephesus, Aspendos, Hattusa), as do
  the operators of the Galata Tower, the Maiden's Tower and the Cistern. Those are rules for
  cameras on the grounds. They make no claim over a picture painted from words, so these places
  follow the ruling on Poland's museums. Drones are banned at the sites, so no view is aerial.
- **No sacred places or tombs:**
  - Hagia Sophia, every mosque and minaret (including in Istanbul's skyline, which stays out
    of frame), Sumela, the Göreme and Ihlara rock churches, Akdamar and the Mevlana;
  - Göbekli Tepe and Hattusa's Yazılıkaya, both sanctuaries;
  - Şanlıurfa's sacred fish, and the whirling dervishes (a rite, and people);
  - Nemrut's heads and the Lycian rock tombs (Dalyan, Myra, Kekova's sarcophagi).
  - **Ephesus is a hold.** The Library of Celsus was built (about 117 AD) as Celsus's
    monument, and his sarcophagus lies in a crypt beneath it. The postcard shows only the
    library front, with no inscriptions, but it is a tomb by purpose. If the lead says no,
    Ölüdeniz, painted from the beach, takes board 309.
- **Atatürk and flags.** No image or statue of Atatürk (protected by Law 5816), no Anıtkabir,
  and no flags: the Galata and Maiden's Towers usually fly one, and here they don't.
- **No borders, disputes or painful history:**
  - Ani, Mount Ararat and the Mesopotamian views from Mardin;
  - Hatay, the 2023 earthquake zone;
  - Northern Cyprus;
  - Gallipoli's battlefields, and Kayaköy, emptied in the 1923 population exchange;
  - Hasankeyf, drowned by a dam in 2020;
  - the Bosphorus bridges (modern, and one is renamed for the 2016 coup attempt).
- **Brands:** Cappadocia's balloons fly plain, with no company names or logos. Also out: the
  Bosphorus ferries' livery, Hacı Bekir's Turkish delight, and Efes beer, which is only a
  namesake.
- **No people:** bazaars, tea gardens, simit sellers, fishermen on the Galata Bridge, the
  paragliders over Ölüdeniz, and swimmers in Cleopatra's Pool.

**Library look-alikes.** These postcards take the family of an everyday picture like them,
so the two never share a board:
- Cappadocia's balloons take *Balloon* (hot-air-balloon).
- The Galata Tower takes *Round Tower*.
- The Van cat takes *Ginger Cat*.
- Ayder's plateau takes *Sea of Clouds* (clouds).

The other eleven get families of their own.

**Shipped postcards, not repeated.**
- **Pamukkale's white travertine terraces and their pools are like no shipped postcard.** Hutt
  Lagoon is pink water, and Grand Prismatic a coloured spring.
- **The Maiden's Tower** is a small white tower house on a rock in the strait, not one of the six
  lighthouses.
- **The fairy chimneys** are capped cones of soft tuff, not Bryce's orange hoodoos, and the
  balloons are what the eye goes to.
- **Butterfly Valley needs a check against Keem Bay and Maya Bay.** It is painted from the
  cliff path as a deep V-shaped canyon falling to a pebble beach, with the striped tiger moths
  the valley is named for in front.
- **Aspendos is a Roman theatre with its stage building standing**, not the Colosseum's oval
  (the library's *Colosseum*).
- **Left out as look-alikes:**
  - Ölüdeniz from the air and Salda Lake, like Whitehaven;
  - Kaputaş, a cove like Keem Bay;
  - Tuz Gölü, salt like the Salinas Grandes;
  - Saklıkent, a gorge like the Zion Narrows;
  - Düden's waterfall into the sea;
  - Bodrum and Alaçatı's windmills, like Consuegra;
  - Rize's tea fields (the library's *Tea Terraces*), Istanbul's red tram (*The Red Tram*)
    and Emirgan's tulips (*Tulip Fields*);
  - Patara's turtles (*Sea Turtle*);
  - Kızkalesi, one more castle.

## Left out, and why

- **Hagia Sophia, mosques and minarets, Sumela, the rock churches, Akdamar, the Mevlana,
  Göbekli Tepe, Yazılıkaya, the sacred fish and the dervishes:** sacred.
- **Nemrut and the Lycian rock tombs:** tombs. The Library of Celsus is held for the same reason.
- **Atatürk's image and Anıtkabir, and every flag.**
- **Ani, Ararat, Mardin's views, Hatay, Northern Cyprus, Gallipoli, Kayaköy, Hasankeyf and the
  Bosphorus bridges:** borders, disputes and painful history.
- **Balloon and ferry brands, Hacı Bekir and Efes beer:** brands.
- **Bazaars, tea gardens, sellers, paragliders and swimmers:** people.
- **Look-alikes:** Ölüdeniz from the air, Salda, Kaputaş, Tuz Gölü, Saklıkent, Düden, the
  windmills, Rize's tea, the red tram, Emirgan's tulips, Patara's turtles and Kızkalesi.

That is 4 oil paintings, 3 watercolours, 2 Ottoman miniatures, 2 pastels, 2 gouaches and
2 travel posters. Each style shares the place's culture and period, and none copies a real
picture:
- The Galata Tower's miniature follows Matrakçı Nasuh's view of Istanbul (1537), which shows the
  tower. The Van cat's follows the miniaturists' animals.
- The watercolours follow the late-Ottoman watercolours of Istanbul (Hoca Ali Rıza, d. 1930) and
  the archaeological views of the 19th century.
- The oils follow the Turkish Impressionists of the 1914 generation and their Anatolian towns.
- The posters follow the travel posters of the early Republic in the 1930s.
- The gouaches and pastels are mid-century Turkish illustration.

The miniatures need a new style line, *postcard-miniature*, with no calligraphy, gold border or
figures.

**Stamp t21:** a tulip, the Ottoman flower, in a ring of small carnations from İznik tiles, in a
pistachio-green ink, for Antep's pistachios. That hue (about 90) is free between Italy's olive
(76) and East Coast America's leaf green (111), and it will be paler than both. Turquoise, the
"Turkish stone", is taken twice (Vietnam & Thailand, Canada).

**Cost:** 15 postcards at medium quality are about $0.30, and the stamp about $0.05. With a few
redraws the trip comes to about $0.45.
