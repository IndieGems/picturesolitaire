# Vietnam & Thailand: candidate destinations

Trip 14, boards 196–210 (creator, 2026-11-07). The trip adds no new art except its postcards
and its stamp: every everyday picture comes from the library. Each board has one real place,
shown as a postcard the size of the whole board. A 2×2 part of it, the **crop**, plays on the
board. How postcards are made is in `docs/picture-direction.md` ("Destinations").

**Shapes** (Levels, `docs/difficulty-curve.md`, "Trips 11-15 (plan)"):
- Most boards are 4×5, with a tall 3:5 postcard.
- 205 is 5×5 and the finale, 210, is 6×6, both with a 3:4 postcard.
- 201 is the all-long board: a 4×4 postcard that plays as a two-card strip.

**Tags.** *famous* is a place everyone knows. *hidden gem* is a place few players will know
but they'll want to visit. *silly* is something funny or odd. The mix is 8 famous, 4 gems and
3 silly. Vietnam has 7 places and Thailand 8.

**The rights rules, first** (checked 2026-11-07).
- **Vietnam: nothing modern.** Its 2009 freedom of panorama was narrowed in the 2022 revision
  of the IP law. From 2023, photographing architecture and art in public places for commercial
  use is not covered. So Vietnam's postcards are nature, farmland and old towns:
  - Hội An's houses are 17th–19th century;
  - the basket boats are a folk craft;
  - nothing built recently appears, such as Bà Nà's Golden Bridge, Đà Nẵng's Dragon Bridge or
    Landmark 81.
- **Thailand: buildings may be pictured.** Thai copyright law does not cover pictures of
  architecture (Tilleke & Gibbins; Commons rates Thailand as having freedom of panorama). That
  covers Phuket's shophouses and the railway market's stalls.
- **No royal places or symbols in Thailand.** That rules out:
  - the Grand Palace and Wat Phra Kaew, and royal residences;
  - Doi Inthanon's royal chedis and Hua Hin's royal station;
  - royal portraits, royal flags and the Garuda emblem.
  - The lèse-majesté law (Criminal Code s 112) makes any slip a crime, so none of it is drawn.
- **No temples, shrines or Buddha images in either country.** That rules out:
  - Wat Arun, Wat Pho, Ayutthaya and Sukhothai, Doi Suthep and Chiang Rai's White and Blue
    Temples;
  - spirit houses;
  - Hội An's Japanese Bridge (a temple stands in it), Hà Nội's One Pillar Pagoda and the Temple
    of Literature, Mỹ Sơn and the Marble Mountains;
  - Koh Panyee's mosque.
  - Thailand frowns on Buddha images as decoration, so none appears anywhere.
- **No permit-restricted places:** Sơn Đoòng and Hang Én are entered only by licensed
  expedition. Phong Nha Cave, open to every visitor by boat, is used instead.
- **No war sites or tragedy:** the Củ Chi tunnels, the Bridge on the River Kwai and the Death
  Railway are out.
- **No film tie-ins:**
  - Maya Bay is shown as itself, with nothing from the film made there.
  - Phang Nga's Ko Tapu is known by a film's name, so it is left out.
- **No modern art:** Đà Lạt's Crazy House (by a living architect) is out.
- **No people:** the floating market's boats and the railway market's stalls stand ready with
  no one in sight. Hà Nội's Train Street is left out for the railway market.
- **No brands:** no State Railway of Thailand logo or numbers on the train, no names on the
  boats, and no shop signs in Phuket.
- **Animals: no elephant rides** or captive animals.

**Library look-alikes.** These postcards take the family of an everyday picture like them,
so the two never share a board:
- Hạ Long Bay takes *Karst Peaks*.
- Hội An takes *Lantern Maker*.
- Sa Pa takes *Rice Paddies*.
- Phong Nha takes *Limestone Cave*.
- The basket boats take *Basket Boat*.
- The floating market takes *Floating Market*.
- Erawan takes *Turquoise Falls*' family (copper-teal), as the Fairy Pools did.
- Phuket takes *Shophouses*.
- Bo Sang takes *Paper Parasol*.
- Koh Panyee takes *Stilt House*.

**Earlier postcards it avoids repeating.**
- Mũi Né's red dunes come straight after Morocco's Erg Chebbi, so Mũi Né shows its Fairy
  Stream instead.
- Limestone is the trip's look, so it is kept to Hạ Long, Maya Bay and Koh Panyee. Ninh Bình,
  Railay and Khao Sok are reserves.
- Erawan is the trip's one waterfall, among 75 new postcards.

## Left out, and why

- **The Grand Palace, Wat Phra Kaew, royal residences, royal chedis and stations, portraits,
  flags and the Garuda:** royal.
- **Every temple, pagoda, shrine, spirit house, mosque and Buddha image**, and Hội An's
  Japanese Bridge: sacred.
- **Sơn Đoòng and Hang Én:** licensed expeditions only.
- **Củ Chi, the River Kwai bridge and the Death Railway:** war and tragedy.
- **Ko Tapu:** known by a film's name.
- **Bà Nà's Golden Bridge, the Dragon Bridge, Landmark 81 and the Crazy House:** modern
  works, without a panorama exception in Vietnam.
- **Hà Nội's Train Street:** the railway market takes its joke.
- **Elephant rides:** captive animals.
