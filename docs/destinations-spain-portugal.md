# Spain & Portugal: candidate destinations

Trip 13, boards 181–195 (creator, 2026-11-07). It moved back one trip on 2026-11-07, when East Coast America became trip 11. The trip adds no new art except its postcards
and its stamp: every everyday picture comes from the library. Each board has one real place,
shown as a postcard the size of the whole board. A 2×2 part of it, the **crop**, plays on the
board. How postcards are made is in `docs/picture-direction.md` ("Destinations").

**Shapes.** Levels is setting the slots (`docs/difficulty-curve.md`, "Trips 11-15 (plan)").
Until then they are assumed to be trip 10's:
- Most boards are 4×5, with a tall 3:5 postcard.
- The x5 board (185) is 5×5 and the finale (195) is 6×6, both with a 3:4 postcard.
- 186 is the all-long board: a 4×4 postcard that plays as a two-card strip.

**Tags.** *famous* is a place everyone knows. *hidden gem* is a place few players will know
but they'll want to visit. *silly* is something funny or odd. The mix is 8 famous, 4 gems and
3 silly. Spain has 8 places and Portugal 7.

**The rights rules, first** (checked 2026-11-07).
- **Freedom of panorama.**
  - Spain's is wide. Works permanently in parks, streets, squares and other public ways may be
    reproduced by painting, drawing, photography or film (Ley de Propiedad Intelectual, art.
    35.2). That covers the Plaza de España's tiles too.
  - Portugal's covers works such as architecture and sculpture made to stand permanently in
    public places (Código do Direito de Autor, art. 75.2(q)).
- **The Alhambra is out.** Its Patronato holds some 43 trade marks on the name and says that
  commercial use must be authorised first. It is the Sydney Opera House case: a place that
  licenses its image.
- **Sintra's Pena Palace is free to picture as a generated view.** Parques de Sintra's rules
  are about images captured in its grounds: commercial use of those needs its permission, and
  no photography is allowed inside the palace. So it is shown from outside, from the park's
  hill, with no interior.
- **Belém Tower is the same.** Its rules are for photography and filming on site.
- **Lisbon's trams are a reserve.** Carris sells its own tram merchandise, and I could not
  find whether the tram's look is a registered mark. London's buses were kept out on the same
  doubt.
- **No sacred or religious places.** That rules out:
  - the Sagrada Família, Montserrat, Córdoba's Mezquita, Seville's cathedral and Giralda, and
    Santiago de Compostela;
  - the Jerónimos Monastery, Fátima, Bom Jesus do Monte and Évora's Chapel of Bones;
  - San Juan de Gaztelugatxe, a hermitage on its islet;
  - Toledo's skyline, which its cathedral crowns.
- **No licensed buildings or modern art:**
  - Casa Batlló and Casa Milà license their images.
  - Park Güell is a reserve: the city controls its image use, and I could not settle how far.
  - Out: the Guggenheim Bilbao and Koons's *Puppy*, Valencia's City of Arts and Sciences, and
    Seville's Setas.
  - Out: Dalí's Portlligat and Figueres, whose rights his foundation enforces.
- **Trade marks and brands are kept out:**
  - the Osborne bull;
  - wineries' and port lodges' names (the sails of Porto's rabelo boats are plain);
  - every sign and logo;
  - the year numbers painted on stripped cork oaks.
- **No film tie-ins:** Almería's western film towns are out. The Plaza de España is shown as
  itself, with nothing from the films made there.
- **No people,** and no bulls: the running of the bulls, La Tomatina and the castells are out.
  Gibraltar is not Spain.

**Library look-alikes.** These postcards take the family of an everyday picture like them,
so the two never share a board:
- Consuegra takes *Windmill*.
- Ronda takes *Viaduct*.
- Madrid's Crystal Palace takes *Glasshouse*.
- Costa Nova takes *Beach Huts*' family (sugared-pastel), as the Brighton boxes did.
- La Geria takes *Vineyard*.
- The cork oaks take *Cork Oak*.
- Guadix takes *Cave Houses*.
- Monsanto takes *Mossy Stones*.
- Benagil takes *Limestone Cave*.
- Porto's Ribeira takes *The Tiled House*'s family (sea-blue).
- Pena Palace and Belém Tower take *Castle*'s family, as Chenonceau and Carcassonne did.

**Earlier postcards it avoids repeating.**
- Segovia's aqueduct is a reserve. It is too like Pontcysyllte's aqueduct and Ronda's bridge.
- Cuenca's hanging houses are a reserve. They hang over a gorge as Ronda does.
- The Azores' Sete Cidades is left out: a lake in a crater, like Crater Lake.
- Setenil is left out. It is a third town wedged into rock, after Guadix and Monsanto.

## Left out, and why

- **The Alhambra:** a trade-marked, licensed image.
- **The Sagrada Família, Montserrat, the Mezquita, Seville's cathedral, Santiago, the
  Jerónimos, Fátima, Bom Jesus, Évora's Chapel of Bones, San Juan de Gaztelugatxe and Toledo's
  skyline:** sacred or religious.
- **Casa Batlló, Casa Milà, the Guggenheim and Puppy, the City of Arts and Sciences, the
  Setas, and Dalí's houses:** licensed images or modern art.
- **The Osborne bull:** a trade mark.
- **Almería's western film towns:** film tie-ins.
- **The running of the bulls, La Tomatina and the castells:** people.
- **Sete Cidades and Setenil:** too like Crater Lake, and like Guadix and Monsanto.

That is 4 oil paintings, 3 watercolours, 3 gouaches, 2 travel posters, 2 pastels and one
azulejo panel. The styles share the place's culture and period:
- The travel posters follow Spain's and Portugal's 1920s and 1930s tourism posters.
- The oils follow the Spanish luminists (Sorolla's light).
- The watercolours follow the Romantic travellers who painted Ronda and La Mancha.
- Belém Tower is painted in blue-and-white azulejo, Portugal's own painted tile (`ceramic`).

The board shapes follow Levels' slots.
