# South Africa: candidate destinations

Trip 9, boards 121–135 (creator, 2026-10-27; slots by Levels, `docs/difficulty-curve.md`).
Each board has one real place, shown as a postcard the size of the whole board. A 2×2 part of
it, the **crop**, plays on the board. How postcards are made is in `docs/picture-direction.md`
("Destinations").

**Shapes.** Most boards are 4×5, with a tall 3:5 postcard. A 5×5 board has a 3:4 postcard.
The trip's all-long board (126) is a 4×4 postcard that plays as a wide two-card strip.

**Tags.** *famous* is a place everyone knows. *hidden gem* is a place few players will know
but they'll want to visit. *silly* is something funny or odd. The mix is 8 famous, 4 gems and
3 silly.

**South Africa's rights rules, first.**
- **South Africa has no freedom of panorama** (checked 2026-10-27). The Copyright Act of 1978
  protects buildings and sculptures as artistic works, for the maker's life plus 50 years. Its
  s 15(3) covers only films and broadcasts. The Copyright Amendment Bill would add freedom of
  panorama, but on 26 June 2026 the Constitutional Court sent it back to Parliament, so it is
  not law.
- **So the postcards are landscapes, wildlife and old buildings only.** A building must be one
  whose makers died more than 50 years ago. The Bo-Kaap houses (18th and 19th century), Cape
  Dutch farmhouses and the Cape Point lighthouse (1919) qualify. Nothing modern is shown:
  - no Kirstenbosch Boomslang walkway (2014);
  - no Storms River suspension bridge;
  - no Big Pineapple at Bathurst;
  - no stadiums.
- **No Mandela statues, and no statues or artworks by living or recent artists.** That rules
  out the Union Buildings' statue, the Mandela Capture Site sculpture and the Owl House at
  Nieu-Bethesda.
- **No sacred or restricted sites:**
  - Mapungubwe, a royal burial hill;
  - the San rock art of the Drakensberg and the Cederberg, which is kept out of every frame;
  - Robben Island, a prison reached only by permit boat;
  - the Wild Coast's Hole in the Wall (esiKhaleni), which has a place in Xhosa belief.
- **Table Mountain is a national park.** The rules of SANParks and of the Cableway company are
  for filming on the mountain. A generated view is not filmed there. The cable car and its
  station are kept out, since the Cableway company uses them as its brand.
- **People never look exotic or AI-made.** No postcard shows people. The Bo-Kaap's residents
  have long objected to tourists photographing them and their homes as a spectacle, so the
  street is painted empty: no people, no mosque and no writing.
- **No writing:** signs, numbers, flags and brands are kept out of every postcard.

**Library look-alikes.** These postcards take the family of an everyday picture like them,
so the two never share a board:
- The Bo-Kaap takes *Pastel Row*'s family.
- The ostriches take *Ostrich*.
- The winelands take *Vineyard*.
- Cape Point takes *Cliff Lighthouse*.
- The Wolfberg Arch takes *Sandstone Arch*.
- The meerkats take *Meerkat*.
- Augrabies takes *Ribbon Falls*.
- Namaqualand takes *Wildflower Meadow*'s family.
- The elephants take *Baby Elephant*.
- The hippos take *Hippo*.
- Sani Pass takes *Switchbacks*.

## Left out, and why

- **Mandela statues** and modern public art: the lead's rule, and no freedom of panorama.
- **Mapungubwe, Robben Island, the Hole in the Wall and the San rock art:** sacred or
  restricted.
- **Modern buildings and bridges** (the Boomslang, the Storms River bridge, the Big Pineapple,
  the stadiums): South Africa has no freedom of panorama.
- **Boulders Beach and Muizenberg:** too like Punta Tombo and the Brighton boxes, a trip or two
  before.
