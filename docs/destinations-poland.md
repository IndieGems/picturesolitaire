# Poland: candidate destinations

Trip 20, boards 286–300 (creator, 2026-11-08). Like the other trips from 17 to 22, it adds no
new art except its postcards and its stamp. Each board has one real place, shown as a postcard
the size of the whole board. A 2×2 part of it, the **crop**, plays on the board. How postcards
are made is in `docs/picture-direction.md` ("Destinations").

**Shapes** (Levels, 2026-11-08):
- Most boards are 4×5, with a tall 3:5 postcard.
- 295, the hard board, and the finale, 300, are 5×5, with a 3:4 postcard.
- 291 is the all-long board: a 4×4 postcard whose window is a wide two-card strip.

**Tags.** *famous* is a place everyone knows. *hidden gem* is a place few players will know
but they'll want to visit. *silly* is something funny or odd. The mix is 8 famous, 4 gems and
3 silly. The trip starts on the Baltic in the north-west and runs along the coast. It turns south
through Kuyavia, Greater Poland and Silesia, goes east to Warsaw and Białowieża, and ends in Lesser
Poland: Zalipie, the Tatras and Kraków. That is 11 of the 16 voivodeships, with no more than
three boards in any one.

**The rights rules, first** (checked 2026-11-08).
- **Poland's freedom of panorama is wide.** Article 33(1) of the copyright act lets anyone
  disseminate works permanently on public roads, streets, squares or gardens, commercially too,
  but only the outside view, not an interior. So Zalipie's painted cottages, whose flowers are
  repainted by living painters, may be shown as they stand. Modern sculpture stays out by our
  own rule, so Wrocław's dwarfs (from 2005) and Kraków's fire-breathing dragon (1972) are out.
- **Museums' photo rules.** Malbork, Łazienki and Biskupin are museums that ask for consent and
  a fee to photograph or film commercially on their grounds. They make no claim over paintings
  made elsewhere, and ours are painted from words, seen from public paths. This follows
  the Korean palace ruling; Germany's palaces were out because their operators control the
  image itself. Wieliczka's salt mine is out: its chapels are sacred, and it charges for every
  camera.
- **Brands:** Żubr beer and Żubrówka put a bison on their labels, so the Białowieża bison is a
  wild animal in the forest, with no label or logo. Also out: Wawel chocolate's mark, Toruń's
  gingerbread brands and Sopot's Grand Hotel. The Elbląg Canal's ships carry no names.
- **No sacred places:**
  - Wawel Cathedral, St Mary's Basilica (it stays out of the Cloth Hall's frame), Jasna Góra
    and the wooden churches of the south;
  - Wieliczka's chapels, Kalwaria, and Giewont's summit cross;
  - Kraków's Kazimierz synagogues, and Nikiszowiec's church, which is left out.
- **No WWII or painful history:**
  - Auschwitz and every camp, the Wolf's Lair, Westerplatte, the ghettos and the Warsaw Uprising;
  - Książ's tunnels (Project Riese) and the 1945 ruins;
  - the Palace of Culture (Stalin's gift) and the Gdańsk shipyard's political history;
  - the Smolensk memorials.

  Malbork and Gdańsk's Crane were rebuilt after 1945. They are painted as they stand today.
- **No flags or emblems:** no white-and-red flags and no white eagle. The Poznań goats' tower
  has no coat of arms.
- **No people:** highlanders, raftsmen on the Dunajec, folk costumes, Christmas markets and
  the Wolin Viking festival.
- **No border crossings:** Muskau Park lies across the German border. The Białowieża postcard
  shows the forest, never the Belarus border fence of 2022.

**Library look-alikes.** These postcards take the family of an everyday picture like them,
so the two never share a board:
- Białowieża's wild bison takes *Bison*. The library's is an American bison on the plains; this
  is the European wisent in the forest.
- Łazienki takes *Château by the Lake* (chateau-lake).
- Zalipie takes *Painted House* (painted-facade).
- Malbork takes *Hilltop Castle* (castle).
- The Poznań goats take *Mountain Goat*.
- Gdańsk's Crane takes *Canal House*: tall gabled fronts on the water.

The other nine get families of their own. The two town halls, Wrocław and Poznań, share
*town-hall*.

**Shipped postcards, not repeated.**
- **Malbork is red brick.** Every castle shipped so far is stone or white: Carcassonne,
  Edinburgh, Pfalzgrafenstein and the rest. It is a vast brick fortress of the Teutonic Knights
  above a river.
- **Łazienki is not Chenonceau.** It is painted from the front: a square classical palace with
  a colonnade and statues on its roof, standing on an island. Chenonceau's gallery strides over
  a river on arches.
- **Zalipie is painted flowers on whitewashed wooden cottages**, not a row of pastel houses and
  not Guarda's grey sgraffito.
- **The Crooked Forest is pines bent sideways at the foot of their trunks.** It isn't the
  Dark Hedges' arching avenue.
- **Left out as look-alikes:**
  - pastel rows, of which seven are already shipped: Warsaw's Old Town square, Zamość and
    Gdańsk's Long Market;
  - Morskie Oko, a mountain lake like Moraine Lake and Lac Blanc;
  - Łeba's moving dunes, like the Dune du Pilat;
  - the Hercules' Club at Ojców and the Table Mountains' rocks, like Vila Velha's Cup;
  - Hel's seals, like Kaikoura's;
  - Żywkowo's storks, like El Badi's;
  - Biebrza's elk, like Mac the Moose;
  - Toruń's Leaning Tower and gingerbread, and Sopot's pier, which the library has (*Leaning
    Tower*, *Gingerbread House*, *The Pier*).

## Left out, and why

- **Auschwitz, the camps, the Wolf's Lair, Westerplatte, the ghettos, the Uprising, Książ's
  tunnels, the Palace of Culture, the shipyard and Smolensk:** WWII and painful history.
- **Wawel, St Mary's, Jasna Góra, the wooden churches, Wieliczka, Kalwaria, Giewont's cross and
  the synagogues:** sacred.
- **Wrocław's dwarfs and Kraków's dragon statue:** modern sculpture.
- **Żubr, Żubrówka, Wawel chocolate, Toruń's gingerbread brands and the Grand Hotel:** brands.
- **Muskau Park and the Belarus border fence:** borders.
- **Highlanders, raftsmen, costumes and festivals:** people.
- **Look-alikes:** pastel rows, Morskie Oko, Łeba, Ojców, the Table Mountains, Hel, Żywkowo,
  Biebrza, Toruń and Sopot.

That is 3 oil paintings, 3 watercolours, 3 travel posters, 2 gouaches, 2 naïf paintings and
2 pastels. Each style shares the place's culture and period, and none copies a real picture:
- Łazienki's oil follows Bernardo Bellotto's views of Warsaw from the 1770s, the palace's own
  decade. They were later used to rebuild the city.
- The bison's oil follows Józef Chełmoński's Polish countryside, and Malbork's the Romantic
  castle views of the 19th century.
- The pastels follow Young Poland: Wyspiański's pastels of Kraków and Wyczółkowski's of the
  Tatras.
- The posters follow the Polish travel and spa posters of the 1930s and the Polish poster school.
- Nikiszowiec's naïf painting follows its own miner-painters, the Janów group. Zalipie's follows
  the village's painted flowers.
- The watercolours and gouaches are 19th- and 20th-century Polish views.

**Stamp t20:** a white stork on its nest, Poland being home to about a quarter of the world's
white storks, in a ring of small paper-cut (*wycinanki*) flowers, in raspberry ink. That hue
(about 315) is free between France's purple (294) and South Africa's red (337).

**Cost:** 15 postcards at medium quality are about $0.30, and the stamp about $0.05. With a few
redraws the trip comes to about $0.45.
