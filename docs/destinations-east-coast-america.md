# East Coast America: candidate destinations

Trip 11, boards 151–165 (creator, 2026-11-07: "get another American region in there, since it
is and will probably stay our main audience"). It goes before New Zealand, so the five trips
after it each move back one, to boards 166–240. Like them, it adds no new art except its
postcards and its stamp: every everyday picture comes from the library. Each board has one
real place, shown as a postcard the size of the whole board. A 2×2 part of it, the **crop**,
plays on the board. How postcards are made is in `docs/picture-direction.md` ("Destinations").

**Shapes** (Levels, 2026-11-07):
- Most boards are 4×5, with a tall 3:5 postcard. The finale, 165, is 4×5 too.
- 155 and 160, the hard boards, are 5×5, with a 3:4 postcard.
- 156 is the all-long board: a 4×4 postcard whose window is a wide two-card strip.

**Tags.** *famous* is a place everyone knows. *hidden gem* is a place few players will know
but they'll want to visit. *silly* is something funny or odd. The mix is 8 famous, 4 gems and
3 silly. The trip runs down the coast from Maine to Florida, one board per state, in 14 states
and Washington, D.C. Florida, the biggest holiday state, has two boards. New York, New Jersey
and Virginia have none, because Travel America (trip 1) already has places in each.

**The rights rules, first** (checked 2026-11-07).
- **Buildings are free to picture.** US law lets anyone picture a building seen from a public
  place (17 U.S.C. § 120(a)), whatever its age. Statues and other artworks have no such
  exception, so a statue must be out of copyright (made before 1931) or not shown. The
  Jefferson Memorial's statue (1947) is therefore left in the dark behind the columns.
- **Postcards that need a check:**
  - The Flying Horse Carousel's horses were carved about 1876, so they are long out of
    copyright.
  - Portland Head Light, owned by the town of Cape Elizabeth, has no trademark or licence on
    its image that I could find.
  - Acorn Street is a private lane, but pictured from the public street it is a building
    like any other.
- **No brands or licensed places:** Grand Central, Fallingwater, the Biltmore Estate and
  Colonial Williamsburg are out, along with Hershey, Kennedy Space Center and NASA's insignia,
  Weeki Wachee's mermaids and Rhode Island's Big Blue Bug. Their owners trademark or license
  their image, or the place is a company's mascot or show. Nauset Light is out because it
  is a potato-chip brand's logo.
- **No flags, signs or lettering:** Independence Hall and the Jefferson Memorial lose their
  flags and signs. The Liberty Bell is out because of its inscription, and Key West's
  Southernmost Point buoy because it is all lettering.
- **No sacred places, tragedies or painful history:**
  - Plymouth Rock is where the Wampanoag hold their National Day of Mourning.
  - The Aquinnah cliffs are Wampanoag tribal land.
  - Katahdin is sacred to the Penobscot.
  - Also out: churches and white-steepled village greens, Salem's witch-trial sites, the
    battlefields, Arlington and the 9/11 memorial, Stone Mountain's Confederate carving, and
    Centralia's mine fire.
  - Monticello, Mount Vernon and the plantation oak avenues (Wormsloe, Boone Hall) are left
    out too: people were enslaved there, and the foundations that run the houses control their
    image.
- **No people:** the carousel has no riders and the streets are empty. Amish country is out:
  the Amish ask not to be photographed. The Chincoteague pony swim is out too, because it is a
  crowd and the ponies belong to the town's fire company.

**Library look-alikes.** These postcards take the family of an everyday picture like them,
so the two never share a board:
- Portland Head Light takes *Cliff Lighthouse*.
- The Albany Covered Bridge takes *Covered Bridge*.
- The sugarhouse takes *Log Cabin*, a snowy cabin with smoke.
- Acorn Street takes *Front Stoop* (brick-city).
- The Flying Horse Carousel takes *Carousel Horse*.
- The Thimble Islands take *Islet*.
- The horseshoe crabs take *Hermit Crab*.
- Assateague's horses take *Shetland Pony*, as Iceland's horses do.
- The Tidal Basin's cherry blossoms take *Blossom Lane* (pink-bloom).
- The Venus flytraps take *Flytrap*.
- Rainbow Row takes *Pastel Row* (sugared-pastel).
- Driftwood Beach takes *Driftwood*.
- The burrowing owls take *Paper Owl*.
- The Everglades alligator takes *Crocodile*.

**Trips 1 and 4, not repeated.**
- Trip 1's East Coast places are left out: the Statue of Liberty, Niagara Falls, Letchworth,
  Lucy the Elephant, Mabry Mill, Castillo de San Marcos and the French Quarter.
- Look-alikes of trip 1's and 4's postcards are out:
  - Waterfalls: Great Falls, Kent Falls and Ricketts Glen, like Niagara, Letchworth and
    Palouse Falls.
  - Star forts: Fort McHenry and the Dry Tortugas' Fort Jefferson, like the Castillo.
  - Natural Bridge, like Delicate Arch.
  - The Flume, like Zion Narrows.
  - Providence Canyon, like the Grand Canyon.
  - Buildings shaped like things: the Haines Shoe House and the Big Duck, like Lucy, the
    Teapot and the Dog Bark Park Inn.
  - Acadia's Jordan Pond, like Emerald Bay and Crater Lake.
- **No autumn colour.** Mabry Mill's red maples, the Cabot Trail and Arrowtown already have it,
  so the covered bridge is painted in summer green.
- Only one lighthouse, Portland Head. Cape Hatteras is a reserve.
- Rainbow Row is the only pastel row of houses. Florida's flamingos (the Camargue) and the
  Seven Mile Bridge (five viaducts already) are out.

## Left out, and why

- **Trip 1's places:** the Statue of Liberty, Niagara Falls, Letchworth, Lucy the Elephant,
  Mabry Mill, Castillo de San Marcos and the French Quarter.
- **Look-alikes of trips 1 and 4:** waterfalls, star forts, Natural Bridge, the Flume,
  Providence Canyon, Jordan Pond, the Haines Shoe House and the Big Duck.
- **Grand Central, Fallingwater, the Biltmore and Colonial Williamsburg:** their image is
  trademarked or licensed.
- **Hershey, Kennedy Space Center, Weeki Wachee and the Big Blue Bug:** brands and shows.
  **Nauset Light** is a brand's logo.
- **Plymouth Rock, the Aquinnah cliffs and Katahdin:** sacred, or a day of mourning.
- **Churches and steepled greens, Salem, the battlefields, Arlington and the 9/11 memorial:**
  sacred places and tragedies.
- **Stone Mountain:** a Confederate carving.
- **Monticello, Mount Vernon and the plantation avenues:** places of slavery whose image their
  foundations control.
- **Amish country and the Chincoteague pony swim:** people.
- **The Liberty Bell and the Southernmost Point buoy:** lettering.
- **Centralia:** an underground fire still burns there.
- **Autumn foliage, a second lighthouse, flamingos and the Seven Mile Bridge:** earlier
  postcards have them.

That is 4 oil paintings, 3 watercolours, 3 gouaches, 2 pastels, 2 travel posters and a folk
painting. Each style shares the place's culture and period, and none copies a real picture:
- Portland Head's watercolour follows the 1920s American watercolours of the Maine coast.
- The covered bridge's oil follows the White Mountain painters of the 1850s, the bridge's own
  decade.
- The sugarhouse follows New England folk paintings of sugaring off: flat, bright and naive.
- Acorn Street's oil follows Childe Hassam's Boston streets.
- The Thimble Islands' pastel follows the Connecticut Impressionists.
- Independence Hall's oil follows 19th-century American views of the State House.
- The posters follow the 1930s national-park and railroad posters.
- The flytraps follow the Carolina naturalists' watercolours (Catesby, Bartram), without
  their engraved hatching.
- Rainbow Row's watercolour follows the Charleston Renaissance painters of the 1920s.
- The alligator follows Martin Johnson Heade's Florida marsh paintings.
- The carousel, the crabs and the owls are mid-century American gouache illustration.

**Stamp t11:** a lighthouse on a rocky point with curling waves, in a ring of small scallop
shells, in a fresh leaf-green ink. The game's stamps leave that hue free, between Italy's olive
and New Zealand's emerald.

**Cost:** 15 postcards at medium quality are about $0.24, and the stamp about $0.05. With a
few redraws the trip comes to about $0.40.
