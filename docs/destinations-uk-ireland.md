# UK & Ireland: candidate destinations

Trip 10, boards 136–150 (creator, 2026-10-27; slots by Levels, `docs/difficulty-curve.md`).
Each board has one real place, shown as a postcard the size of the whole board. A 2×2 part of
it, the **crop**, plays on the board. How postcards are made is in `docs/picture-direction.md`
("Destinations").

**Shapes.** Most boards are 4×5, with a tall 3:5 postcard. A 5×5 board, and 150, the 6×6
finale, have a 3:4 postcard. The trip's all-long board (141) is a 4×4 postcard that plays as a
tall two-card strip.

**Tags.** *famous* is a place everyone knows. *hidden gem* is a place few players will know
but they'll want to visit. *silly* is something funny or odd. The mix is 8 famous, 4 gems and
3 silly. Every nation gets a place: England 5, Scotland 4, Wales 1, Northern Ireland 2 and
Ireland 3.

**The rights rules, first** (checked 2026-10-27).
- **Freedom of panorama covers buildings and sculptures in public places.** In the UK that is
  the CDPA 1988, s 62; in Ireland, the CRRA 2000, s 93. It does not cover murals or other flat
  art.
- **Trade marks still apply, so these are kept out:**
  - the London Eye, which Merlin licenses;
  - red phone boxes, whose shapes BT registered, and which carry a crown;
  - London buses and black cabs;
  - every sign, logo and brand.
- **No royal insignia.** Royal arms, crowns, cyphers and the Royal Standard are kept out. So
  are Parliament's crowned portcullis, a royal badge it uses on its own goods, every flag, and
  the gates of Buckingham Palace.
- **The Palace of Westminster is free to picture.** The building was rebuilt from 1840, so it
  is out of copyright, and s 62 covers it anyway. Parliament's rules are about its own
  photographs, its bells' recordings and filming on the estate. Its 2016 notice "Big Ben is not
  a billboard" is against projections on the tower. So the palace is shown by day from across
  the Thames, with no emblems, flags, buses or the London Eye.
- **Stonehenge is free to picture.**
  - English Heritage's visitor terms forbid commercial photos or filming *at* the site
    without its written permission, and drones over it.
  - In 2010 it said it does not control the copyright of all images of Stonehenge.
  - A generated view is not taken at the site.
  - The stones are a monument, not a closed sacred site, though modern pagans gather there at
    the solstices.
  - So it is shown by day, from outside the circle, with no people, ceremony, fences or
    visitor path.
- **National Trust places are free to picture as generated views.** The Giant's Causeway is
  one. The Trust's permission and fees cover photography and filming *on* its land, and
  reproducing the Trust's own pictures. No NT signs, oak-leaf logo or visitor centre appear.
- **Edinburgh Castle and Urquhart Castle** belong to Historic Environment Scotland. Its
  permits are for filming on site. The royal arms over the castle gate and the flags are kept
  out.
- **No sacred, religious or permit-restricted places.** That rules out:
  - Skellig Michael, an early monastery whose landing is licensed;
  - Newgrange and the Poulnabrone dolmen, which are tombs;
  - Kylemore Abbey, home to a community of nuns;
  - the Rock of Cashel.
- **No film or TV tie-ins.** The Dark Hedges are shown as a beech avenue, with nothing from
  the series filmed there. Glenfinnan's viaduct is left out for its film train.
- **No people:** every postcard shows a place, an animal or both.

**Library look-alikes.** These postcards take the family of an everyday picture like them,
so the two never share a board:
- Stonehenge takes *Standing Stones*.
- Durdle Door takes *Sea Stack*.
- The Pontcysyllte Aqueduct takes *Viaduct*.
- The Dark Hedges take *Beech Woods*.
- The Giant's Causeway takes *Basalt Columns*.
- The Connemara sheep take *Lamb*.
- The Dublin doors take *Yellow Door*.
- The Highland cow takes *Highland Cow*.
- The Fairy Pools take *Turquoise Falls*' family.
- Urquhart Castle and Edinburgh Castle take *Castle*.

## Left out, and why

- **The London Eye:** licensed by its owner.
- **Skellig Michael, Newgrange, Poulnabrone, Kylemore Abbey and the Rock of Cashel:** sacred,
  religious or licensed landing.
- **Glenfinnan Viaduct:** its film train, and a viaduct was in trip 7.
- **Slieve League and Tenby:** too like Moher and the Bo-Kaap.
- **Hadrian's Wall's Sycamore Gap:** the tree was felled in 2023.
