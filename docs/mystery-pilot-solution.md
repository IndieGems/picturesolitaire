# Murder on the Melusine: the solution

**Professor Henri Duval.** The proof is board 15, the passenger's photo.

1. **The watch was staged.** It says 9:45, but Brandt was alive at 10:25: he
   opened the door and asked for a second cup (board 10). The killer set the
   watch to 9:45, when Henri sat in the front row in front of everyone. That
   time also points at Dr Fehr, who was alone until 10:15, and at his own
   sleeping drops (boards 3, 9).
2. **The killer was a friend, and came between 10:25 and 11:05.** Brandt asked
   for a cup "for a friend". By 11:05 the self-locking door went unanswered, so
   nobody who came later could get in (boards 7, 10).
3. **Everyone but Henri is accounted for in that window.** Clara was on stage
   until 11:00 and then signing programmes (boards 2, 6). The doctor was with
   Mrs Kovács until 11:10 (board 11). Margit was ashore until 11:15 (board 12).
4. **Henri lied.** He says he sat in the front row all of Part 2. At 10:35 the
   front-row chair with his mustard-yellow scarf is empty (boards 5, 15).
5. **The motive was forgery.** The waltz was in fresh green ink (board 14). Two
   suspects write in green, Henri and the doctor (boards 5, 11), but only Henri
   had the opportunity. The poison went into the fresh pot brought at 10:25
   (boards 9, 10), so nothing before then matters. Henri forged it, sold it to his friend, and knew the
   Strauss archive would expose it in Vienna the next day (board 1). He drugged
   the tea, took the manuscript and threw it in the river, where a scrap caught on
   the anchor chain.

**Red herrings.**
- Clara had the strongest motive: the unsigned will would have cut her out
  (boards 2, 13). She also had the interval free, but by 10:25 she was back on
  stage.
- Dr Fehr had the means and a weak early alibi (boards 3, 8, 9).
- Margit made a threat (board 4).

**Accepted answers.** The culprit is Henri. The proving picture is the photo
(board 15). The scrap (14) or the bells (10) earn the win but not the gold stamp.
