# Germany, Austria & Switzerland: candidate destinations

Trip 18, boards 256–270 (creator, 2026-11-08). Like the other trips from 17 to 22, it adds no
new art except its postcards and its stamp. Each board has one real place, shown as a postcard
the size of the whole board. A 2×2 part of it, the **crop**, plays on the board. How postcards
are made is in `docs/picture-direction.md` ("Destinations").

**Shapes** (Levels, 2026-11-08):
- Most boards are 4×5, with a tall 3:5 postcard. The finale, 270, is 4×5 too.
- 260 and 265, the hard boards, are 5×5, with a 3:4 postcard.
- 261 is the all-long board: a 4×4 postcard whose window is a wide two-card strip.

**Tags.** *famous* is a place everyone knows. *hidden gem* is a place few players will know
but they'll want to visit. *silly* is something funny or odd. The mix is 8 famous, 4 gems and
3 silly. All three countries appear: Germany has 7 boards, Switzerland 4 and Austria 4. The trip
starts at Hamburg and the Baltic and goes through Berlin, Saxony and the Rhine to Bavaria. It
crosses Switzerland from west to east, then follows the Alps through Austria to Vienna.

**The rights rules, first** (checked 2026-11-08).
- **Freedom of panorama.** All three countries let anyone picture a building or artwork that
  stands permanently in a public place:
  - **Germany** (§ 59 UrhG) names painting outright, but only the view from the public road,
    path or square: never an aerial view, a courtyard or an interior.
  - **Austria** (§ 54 UrhG) and **Switzerland** (Art. 27 URG) allow the same, in two
    dimensions.
- **Palaces that control their image are out.** Germany's Federal Court ruled in 2013 that
  the Prussian Palaces Foundation may stop commercial pictures made in its gardens (Sanssouci).
  The Bavarian Palace Administration (Neuschwanstein, Linderhof, Herrenchiemsee) and Schönbrunn's
  and the Belvedere's operators charge for commercial images too. None of their palaces
  appears.
- **Trade marks and brands:**
  - Neuschwanstein's name is an EU trade mark of the Free State of Bavaria, upheld by the EU
    Court of Justice in 2019. Its look is also Disney's castle. The library's *Castle* is
    already Neuschwanstein, so it is out three times over.
  - Also out: the Ampelmännchen, Oktoberfest, the Sachertorte, Toblerone's Matterhorn, the Swiss
    Army knife, the SBB railway clock (a licensed design), the Zeppelin, Steiff, and the Glacier
    Express and the Jungfraujoch's "Top of Europe".
  - Vienna's Giant Ferris Wheel is its operator's attraction and brand, and the setting of
    *The Third Man*.
  - Trains, boats and beach chairs carry no logos, liveries or numbers.
- **Flags and arms.** Switzerland's coat-of-arms law protects the Swiss cross, so no flag, and no
  cross on a dog's barrel. Bern's bear is on the city's arms, but the postcard shows live bears.
  The opera flies no banners.
- **Film and book tie-ins are out.** That means *The Sound of Music*'s Salzburg (the Mirabell
  steps and the Hellbrunn gazebo), *The Third Man*'s Vienna and Maienfeld's "Heididorf". Iseltwald's
  pier on Lake Brienz, from a K-drama, now charges a fee for each photo. Guarda is pictured for its
  houses: the postcard doesn't name the *Schellen-Ursli* picture book set there.
- **No churches or sacred places.** Cologne Cathedral, Dresden's Frauenkirche, St Stephen's,
  the Karlskirche, Melk, Einsiedeln, the Wieskirche and Ramsau's church are out, and so are
  Königssee's St Bartholomä and Hallstatt's spire. The Great St Bernard Hospice is a monastery,
  so it is left out of the dogs' postcard. Oberammergau's painted houses are mostly saints and the
  Passion.
- **No WWII or Nazi-era sites, and no painful history.** That rules out:
  - the Eagle's Nest and Obersalzberg, the Nuremberg rally grounds, Prora on Rügen, the
    Externsteine (misused by the SS), Berlin's 1936 Olympic Stadium and Tempelhof's terminal;
  - Vienna's Heldenplatz (the 1938 Anschluss speech) and every camp memorial;
  - the Berlin Wall, its murals (the East Side Gallery) and Checkpoint Charlie;
  - Lucerne's Lion Monument, a memorial to the Swiss Guards killed in 1792.

  The Brandenburg Gate (1791) is painted as the unity symbol it now is, from the west, with no
  Wall.
- **Modern art and buildings are out:** Hundertwasser House, the Elbphilharmonie, the 1953
  Bremen Town Musicians and Bregenz's floating stage. Eisriesenwelt bans photography inside.
- **No people:** Christmas markets, Carnival masks, Krampus runs, the alpine cattle drives with
  their herders, yodellers and alphorn players.

**Library look-alikes.** These postcards take the family of an everyday picture like them,
so the two never share a board:
- The Speicherstadt takes *Green Spires* (copper-teal): copper roofs over a canal and bridges.
- The beach chairs take *Beach Huts* (sugared-pastel).
- The Rakotz Bridge takes *Stone Bridge* (arch-bridge).
- The Schwebebahn takes *Monorail*.
- Pfalzgrafenstein takes *Castle*.
- Rothenburg takes *Half-Timbered House*.
- The Chapel Bridge takes *Covered Bridge*.
- Guarda takes *Painted Facade*.
- The Golden Roof takes *Carved Balcony Window* (palace-window).
- The marmots take *Meerkat*: both sit up on their haunches.
- Grüner See takes copper-teal, the emerald-water family of the Fairy Pools and Erawan.

The Brandenburg Gate, the Bern bears, the St Bernard dogs and the State Opera get families of
their own.

**Shipped postcards, not repeated.**
- **Rothenburg's Plönlein is not Colmar.** Colmar is houses over a canal; the Plönlein is one
  yellow house at a fork in a cobbled street, between two gate towers, with no water.
- **The Chapel Bridge is not the Albany Covered Bridge.** It is a long footbridge across a wide
  city river, with an octagonal stone tower, flower boxes and Mount Pilatus. Albany is a short
  dark bridge in a forest.
- **Pfalzgrafenstein is the only castle**, and it is not like the nine already shipped. It is a
  white toll castle on an island in the middle of the Rhine, shaped like a ship.
- The Speicherstadt is dark red brick warehouses standing straight in the water. It is not
  Nyhavn's coloured gables.
- **Grüner See is told apart by what's under the water:** a park bench and a footpath, drowned
  every spring.
- The marmots lie on a sunny rock in an alpine meadow, not standing guard in the desert like the
  Kalahari meerkats.
- **Left out as look-alikes:**
  - the Matterhorn, Hallstatt (the library's *Lake Village*), Neuschwanstein, the cuckoo clock,
    garden gnomes, the pretzel, the Lüneburg Heath (*Heather Moor*) and the cattle drives
    (*Cow Parade*);
  - Rügen's chalk cliffs, like Møns Klint;
  - the Wadden Sea, like Mont-Saint-Michel's tide flats;
  - the waterfalls: the Rhine Falls, Lauterbrunnen, Krimml and Triberg;
  - the viaducts: Landwasser and Göltzsch, and the Bastei, like Ronda;
  - the glaciers: the Aletsch and the Pasterze, like Franz Josef;
  - the rowboat lakes: Königssee, Eibsee, Oeschinensee and Gosausee;
  - the vineyards: the Moselle, Lavaux and the Wachau;
  - more castles: Heidelberg, Eltz, Cochem, Hohenzollern, Chillon and Hohensalzburg;
  - the Grossglockner road, like Sani Pass;
  - the Creux du Van, like the Amphitheatre;
  - Geneva's Jet d'Eau, a white jet like Pohutu;
  - the Lipizzaners, because two horse postcards are already shipped.

## Left out, and why

- **Neuschwanstein, Sanssouci, Linderhof, Herrenchiemsee, Schönbrunn and the Belvedere:**
  palaces whose operators control their image. Neuschwanstein is also a trade mark and Disney's
  look.
- **The Matterhorn, Hallstatt, the cuckoo clock, gnomes, the pretzel, the heath and the cattle
  drives:** the library has the same pictures.
- **The Ampelmännchen, Oktoberfest, Sacher, Toblerone, the Swiss Army knife, the SBB clock,
  the Zeppelin, Steiff, the Glacier Express, the Jungfraujoch and the Giant Ferris Wheel:**
  brands.
- ***The Sound of Music*, *The Third Man*, Heidi and Iseltwald's K-drama pier:** tie-ins.
- **Cathedrals, churches, abbeys and the St Bernard Hospice:** sacred.
- **The Eagle's Nest, Nuremberg, Prora, the Externsteine, the Olympic Stadium, Tempelhof,
  Heldenplatz, the camps, the Wall and Checkpoint Charlie:** the Nazi era and painful history.
  **Lucerne's Lion Monument** is a memorial.
- **Hundertwasser House, the Elbphilharmonie, the Bremen Town Musicians and Bregenz's stage:**
  modern art.
- **Eisriesenwelt:** no photography.
- **Look-alikes of shipped postcards:** Rügen, the Wadden Sea, the waterfalls, viaducts and the
  Bastei, glaciers, rowboat lakes, vineyards, more castles, the Grossglockner road, the Creux du
  Van, the Jet d'Eau and the Lipizzaners.

That is 4 oil paintings, 3 watercolours, 3 gouaches, 3 travel posters and 2 pastels. Each style
shares the place's culture and period, and none copies a real picture:
- The Speicherstadt's oil follows the Hamburg harbour painters around 1900.
- The Brandenburg Gate's oil follows Eduard Gaertner's crisp Berlin views of the 1830s.
- The St Bernard's oil follows 19th-century Swiss animal painting.
- The State Opera's oil follows the Ringstrasse era of the 1870s–80s.
- The posters:
  - The beach chairs follow the German seaside-resort posters of the 1920s.
  - The Schwebebahn follows the German *Sachplakat* posters of the 1900s, its own decade.
  - The bears follow the Swiss travel posters of the 1930s.
- The watercolours:
  - Pfalzgrafenstein follows the Romantic Rhine views of the 1820s–40s.
  - The Rakotz Bridge follows the 1880s park views of its own time.
  - The Golden Roof follows Rudolf von Alt's watercolours of Innsbruck.
- The gouaches are mid-century Swiss and Austrian illustration.
- The pastels follow the turn-of-the-century painters who discovered Rothenburg, and the soft
  water of Grüner See.

**Stamp t18:** an edelweiss in a ring of small fir sprigs, the flower of all three countries'
Alps, in a violet ink. That hue (about 272) is free between Morocco's blue (251) and France's purple
(294).

**Cost:** 15 postcards at medium quality are about $0.30, and the stamp about $0.05. With a few
redraws the trip comes to about $0.45.
