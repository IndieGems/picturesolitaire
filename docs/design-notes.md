## Rulings in full (moved from AGENTS.md, 2026-10-30)

The full text of rulings that `AGENTS.md` now states in a sentence or two, as they stood when moved:

- **Art follows the chosen look.** `design/concepts/game-l.png` is the style reference for every piece of art, however it is made (an image generator, sprites drawn in code, three.js materials, Blender): open it before making any. For a generated image pass it as the reference and draw only the new subject from it; for a pose or variant of an existing sprite, use that sprite instead. `style.visual` in `design.json` says the look in words, and where they disagree with the picture the words win. **Except the mood: the game is sunny** (creator, 2026-10-28, after the store's bright suitcase art and travel-poster logo, `.gemarcade/hero/`). The default table is a bright, sunny travel scene in their colours. game-l.png's snowy dusk desk is now the Winter Dusk table, December's suitcase prize (it replaced Winter Market, whose art is kept for a later winter event), and game-l.png stays the reference for the chrome: frosted glass, the button materials, the type.
- **Except the puzzle pictures themselves.** game-l.png governs the table and the chrome only. Each picture on the cards is a photograph or a painted illustration in its own medium, at full fidelity: never generate one from game-l.png, and never tint, filter or frame-colour it.
- **Pictures are clear, close and varied** (creator, 2026-10-24, comparing ours with Jigsaw Drop: `design/references/ours-level-3-too-busy.png` vs `jigsaw-drop-level-1[34]*.png`). One readable subject, framed close, on a calm background, so every card shows a recognisable part. **At most one picture per level is atmospheric** (creator, 2026-10-28, capped 2026-10-29: a landscape, a distant scene, impressionistic or busier, for variety; the early pictures failed only because ALL were like that), in every lane but the Souvenirs; the clear pictures they displaced stock the next trips. Mix art styles (photo, 3D render, flat and retro illustration, low-poly, painting) and vary subjects within a board, but **new pictures lean clean** (creator, 2026-10-29, after Jigsaw Drop's crisper boards, `design/references/jigsaw-drop-crisp-*.png`): a bright subject on a deep plain ground, big simple shapes cropped close, smooth shading, little texture; heavy brush, paper or stitch texture is an occasional accent, since it turns to noise at card size. This overrides design.json's "pictures on one board share colours and subjects on purpose": no look-alike families on a board. Never colour-code pictures either. **No new flower pictures** (creator, 2026-10-29: the library has far too many): none whose sole subject is a flower; flowers within a scene (a meadow, a market stall) are fine. A style and its subject share a culture and period (creator, 2026-10-29: a ukiyo-e kimono woman driving a Western sleigh), so name a subject that fits the style, or the generator invents a mash-up. Art's `docs/picture-direction.md` has the detail. **Pictures rarely repeat** (creator, 2026-10-25; JD players notice repeats only after 200+ levels): no picture on two of boards 1–13, then none back within ~15 boards, unseen pictures first; the generator enforces it. **People pictures vary, and never look AI-made** (creator, 2026-10-29, after too many turned away, `design/references/ours-people-turned-away-*.png`, then a pilot all framed alike, face top-left and torso up): many show the face (front, three-quarter, glancing off-frame, eye contact allowed) beside profiles and backs, and the framing varies just as much: a full figure in its setting, two people together, from above or below, a close crop cut by the edge, layered depth; never the same set-up twice running. The people are as varied, with many more people of colour, particularly African Americans (creator, 2026-10-29). Still banned: the AI look (creator, 2026-10-25: the same tilted head and posed smile on everyone). Style references: `design/references/jigsaw-drop-level-1[67]*.png`, `painterly-portrait-girl-red-bow.png`. **A picture ships at the size it is drawn** (creator, 2026-10-25, now and ongoing): the board draws a board-sized copy picked for the screen, and only the enlarged view fetches the full picture.
- **Helpers are the player's own stock, carried from board to board** (creator, 2026-10-27, after the mahjong-solitaire game's model): Hint, Undo and Shuffle are Arcade-held game currencies, capped at 10 each, never in the save. The guided first board ends with 3 of each, free; a hard board's first win pays 1 Hint and 1 Undo, and a passport stamp 1 of each; more are bought with Coins (prices in config: Undo 100, Hint 150, Shuffle 250). Restart is always free, with no lives or energy. Board 2 teaches Undo and a staged practice board after board 3 teaches Shuffle, both free. Every board stays winnable with no helper; dev-menu practice spends nothing. Also (creator, 2026-10-27, from Match3Mahjong): the daily's first win each day gives one helper; packs of 3 sell at a small saving, from a shop on home too; after repeated stuck tries on one board the next try starts with a free, announced helper (mercy help, never a quietly easier board).
- **VIP perks, built now behind one switch that is off** (creator, 2026-10-30, `docs/vip.md`). Gem Arcade VIP members get: free unlimited helpers with a short per-button cooldown; a daily archive; the event, now two weeks long and VIP-only, with an event archive; and every Cosmetic to wear (what they bought is kept when VIP ends). The platform can't yet say who holds VIP, so the server decides: "not VIP" on release and test, with a dev-only "Pretend I'm VIP" toggle. Until then no player sees the event, the daily grants none of its items, and Ski Lodge and Winter Dusk show only to players who own them. The challenge route is off home, its code kept, until the creator decides.
- **Play is about finding matches, not admiring pictures** (creator, 2026-10-24, after JD): a dragged piece follows the finger 1:1 with no lift and no stickiness; a finished picture leaves quickly (a slight grow, twinkles, a fast shrink to nothing). Players see pictures on the win card, tap to enlarge. The destination's unfolding postcard (below) and the daily's suitcase item (creator, 2026-10-27: lifted off the board, big, with a starburst and its name, then shrunk into the suitcase) are the two deliberate pauses. The table's weather (petals, snow, leaves) is for home and the menus only: it fades out while a board, its win card or a stamp moment is up (creator, 2026-10-28; src/ui/particles.ts).
- **The game is a travelogue, "Picture Passport"** (creator, 2026-10-25). The campaign is trips of 15 boards (`src/content/levels/trips.json`); finishing a trip stamps the passport, which replaces the albums. Each board has one destination, a real place as a board-sized postcard (`destinations` in `pictures.json`), whose chosen 2×2 window (a long one on an all-long board) is dealt as an ordinary picture; finishing it unfolds the postcard and names the place, then play goes on. A trip is about 8 famous places, 4 hidden gems and 3 silly ones. Postcards are generated and fact-checked against real photos, described in words, never made from someone's photo, and **always an obviously painted style, never photoreal** (creator, 2026-10-28: a generated "photo" of a real place passes itself off as one; a painting leaves leeway), its style suited to the place; the same goes for any library picture of a real landmark; no sacred or permit-restricted sites, modern art, trademarked signs or buildings whose owners license their image (`docs/destinations-america.md`). **No mystery in this game** (creator, 2026-10-26): the Traveling Detective pilot is set aside to become its own game, kept on the git branch `mystery-pilot`; this game doubles down on travel. **Players read "Level", never "Board"** (creator, 2026-10-29): code, docs and these rulings say board.
- **The creator tests on the phone through "the Pit"** (2026-10-24): the website's Dev "Latest/Unstable" list shows the newest web upload of every org game to the whole team, with no promote or store page, and plays it on Dev's config, function and saves. So an upload (`gemctl build upload … --version next-minor`) is the phone build and the team can see it. The game is listed on the Dev catalog (creator, 2026-10-25, build 0.12.0): the Pit has the newest upload, and Dev has the build last promoted there, so promote one to dev when the creator wants it there. Keep a store page on Dev: without one the Pit's tile sticks on a loading page on the phone.
- **The stacks' next card is face up again, on trial** (creator, 2026-10-25, after getting stuck on boards 9 and 10 with card backs: "it would be pure luck to not get into this situation"). A board-tuning switch shows it; card backs (`peek` 0, each table with its own back) stay available behind the switch until the creator decides.
- **Difficulty is a sawtooth, not a ramp** (creator, 2026-10-25): spikes, then relief, then a higher spike, with a bite as early as board 3. The plan and its measured targets are in `docs/difficulty-curve.md`. Harder than the genre's tries targets is fine (creator, 2026-10-27: far fewer levels than Royal Match, and a different kind of game). Hard and super hard boards are marked for the player (creator, 2026-10-25): a board's `mark` in its level data, shown on home, as a short banner on opening, in the top row and on the win card. **About 65% of the hard and finale boards from board 10 are 5×5** (creator, 2026-10-27, after Jigsaw Drop's level 18: a frequent variation, never a rule): two per trip from trip 2, in different slots each trip, keeping each board's difficulty target; and every other finale (30, 60, 90) is 6×6 (creator, 2026-10-27, after Jigsaw Drop's level 21). The list is in `docs/difficulty-curve.md`.
- **Levels are proved data.** Every deal in `src/content/levels/` is written by a `tools/levels/make-*.mts` script and re-proved by `tools/levels/check.mts` in the build (its solver proofs cached by content hash in everyday builds; a release build re-proves all, `--full`); never hand-edit one. A solver "unknown" (out of budget) proves nothing, and such a deal never ships. A relief or lesson board starts safe: any picture finishable from the opening can be finished first, anywhere, and no first move leaves the board stuck (`safeFirstMove`, generate.ts); a hard board may trap from its first move, but every board stays winnable with no helper. An unmarked board is also READABLE (creator, 2026-10-27, after boards 18–19 felt rigged): a player who draws by the face-up stack cards wins it 85%+ of the time, while careless play keeps its target; only hard and super hard boards may hide the way behind a stack's second card, and even there a careful player wins about 1 in 4 or more (creator, 2026-10-27, after board 60 at 1 in 10). Hint sees the hidden cards, so it is the tool for "I can't tell where to match". The makers keep every shipped board; a picture flagged in `src/content/picture-flags.json` fails check.mts, and `tools/levels/redeal.mts` re-deals only the boards that use it.
