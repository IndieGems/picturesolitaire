# France: candidate destinations

Trip 5, boards 61–75 (creator, 2026-10-26). Each board has one real place, shown as a
postcard the size of the whole board. A 2×2 part of it, the **crop**, plays on the board.
The suggested 15 at the end are the ones made; the creator can swap any after seeing them.
How postcards are made is in `docs/picture-direction.md` ("Destinations").

**Shapes.**
- Boards 61–65 and 67–75 are 4×5, so their postcards are a tall 3:5.
- Board 66 is the trip's all-long board: a 4×4 postcard that plays as a two-card strip.

**Tags.** *famous* is a place everyone knows. *hidden gem* is a place few players will
know but they'll want to visit. *silly* is a delightful oddity. The mix is 8 famous, 4
gems and 3 silly.

**France's rights rules, first.**
- **No freedom of panorama for modern works.** A building, sculpture or bridge whose
  author died less than 70 years ago can't be pictured commercially without permission.
  That rules out the Louvre Pyramid, the Pompidou Centre, the Grande Arche and the Millau
  Viaduct.
- **The Eiffel Tower by day only.** The tower (1889) is free, but its night lighting and
  sparkle are protected works.
- **The national estates.** Since 2016, a commercial image of one of the 21 *domaines
  nationaux* needs its manager's permission. These include Chambord, the Louvre and
  Tuileries, Versailles, Fontainebleau, Vincennes, Pierrefonds, the Palais de la Cité (the
  Sainte-Chapelle and Conciergerie), Compiègne and Saint-Germain-en-Laye. All are left out.
  Checked against the decrees of 2017, 2021, 2022 and 2024 (Culture Ministry, 2026-10-26):
  Mont-Saint-Michel, Carcassonne and Chenonceau are not on the list.
- **Private buildings are fine to picture from outside.** French courts let an owner
  object only when a picture causes them real harm (Cour de cassation, 2004).
- **No writing:** shop signs, boat names and carved inscriptions are kept out.

**Library look-alikes.** These postcards take the family of an everyday picture like them,
so the two never share a board:
- Étretat takes *Sea Stack*.
- Mont-Saint-Michel takes *Tide Flats*.
- Ploumanac'h takes *Cliff Lighthouse*.
- Chenonceau and Carcassonne take *Castle*.
- Colmar takes *Half-Timbered*.
- Valensole takes *Lavender*.
- The Verdon takes *Turquoise Falls*' colours.
- The flamingos take *Flamingo*.
- The Dune du Pilat takes *Dune Caravan*'s colours.

*Eiffel Tower* is a library landmark already, so it never plays on a campaign board.

## Left out, and why

- **The Louvre Pyramid, the Pompidou Centre, the Grande Arche, the Millau Viaduct.**
  Modern works, and France has no freedom of panorama.
- **The Eiffel Tower at night.** The lighting is protected.
- **Chambord, the Louvre, Versailles and the other national estates.** Their managers' permission is
  needed for commercial images.
- **The Machines de l'Île elephant, Nantes, and La Maison Picassiette, Chartres.** Modern
  works by artists still in copyright.
- **The Wall of Love and the Moulin Rouge.** Writing, and a famous sign.
- **The house between the rocks, Plougrescant.** A private home whose owners have asked
  visitors to stay away.
- **Lascaux.** The cave is closed, and the visitor replicas are modern works.
