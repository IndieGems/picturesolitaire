# Travel America: candidate destinations

The first trip, boards 1–15 (lead and creator, 2026-10-25). Each board has one real place,
shown as a postcard the size of the whole board. The 2×2 quarter of it that plays on the
board is the **crop**. The creator picks 15 of the 26 below. A suggested 15 is at the end.

**Tags.** *famous* is a place everyone knows. *hidden gem* is a place few players will
know but they'll want to visit. *silly* is a roadside oddity.

**How postcards are made** (creator, 2026-10-25). They are generated, and checked against
real photos of the place:
- **Check the facts against real photos.** Look at photos of the place (Wikimedia
  Commons, the park's own pages) to get the shape of the land, the landmark, the colours
  and the season right. Never feed someone's photo into the generator: describe the place
  in words instead.
- **Vary the styles.** Most postcards are photographic, with some painted and some in
  travel-poster style. People never get the AI look (AGENTS.md).
- The "Can the generator get it right?" column says whether it can draw the place
  faithfully. A place it can't draw is dropped or swapped.

**Tested.** On 2026-10-25 I made four cheap drafts from words alone: Letchworth, Lucy the
Elephant, Mabry Mill and Miners Castle (Pictured Rocks). All four matched real photos in
shape, layout and colour. The Grand Canyon test postcard matches photos from Lipan Point on
the South Rim. The other places are judged by how well known and how often photographed
they are.

**What makes a good crop.** The crop is a quarter of the postcard: half its width and half
its height, on whole cards. It should hold the place's most striking part, with each of
its four cards different. A crop that is all sky, all water or all of one rock makes four
cards alike, and those are hard to match. Every crop below is a guess until there is a
picture to cut. A 4×5 board's postcard is taller (3:5), so generate each one tall and cut
it down to 3:4 for a 4×4 board.

**Rights.** Generating doesn't change what a picture may show:
- Nature, old landmarks and public streets are safe.
- Modern sculpture and art, trademarked signs and buildings whose owners license their
  image are not.
- Keep signs, shop names and boat names out of every postcard. The game writes the place
  name itself.

**Library look-alikes.** Some places are close to a picture already in the everyday library:
- Delicate Arch is close to *Sandstone Arch*.
- Grand Prismatic is close to *Geyser*.
- Haystack Rock is close to *Sea Stack*.
- Mabry Mill is close to *Watermill*.
- Cape Hatteras is close to the two lighthouse pictures.
- Golden Gate is close to *Golden Gate*.

Levels keeps pictures that share a subject off the same board.

## Left out, and why

- **Monument Valley and Antelope Canyon.** Both are Navajo Nation land, with permits for
  commercial photography.
- **Mount Rushmore.** A carved sculpture in the Black Hills, which are sacred to the Lakota.
  **Devils Tower** is sacred to many tribes too.
- **Hollywood sign and the Las Vegas welcome sign.** Both are lettering, and the Hollywood
  sign is a trademark.
- **Empire State Building and the Space Needle.** Their owners license the buildings'
  image.
- **"The Bean" (Chicago), Cadillac Ranch, Carhenge, the Corn Palace.** Modern art and
  murals by living artists.
- **Haleakalā at sunrise.** Sacred, and the sunrise needs a booking.
- **Taos Pueblo.** A living community with its own photography rules.
- **White Sands, the Bonneville Salt Flats and Carlsbad Caverns.** Every card would look
  the same, or the place would look like anywhere.
